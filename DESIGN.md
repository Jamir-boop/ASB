# Design System: ASB

## Overview

**Creative North Star: "Column Flow"**

ASB is a native GNOME session index. The user approved Column Flow and the Workspace-first Comfortable option. Compact keeps one line per session; Comfortable puts the folder above a two-line title. Both use the available window area for rows and columns.

The title and text state carry the main information. Provider marks stay subdued. Attention has a clear label and, when unread, a small dot.

**Key Characteristics:**

- Native GTK4 and Libadwaita controls.
- Dark mode only within ASB.
- Compact 22px rows and Workspace-first Comfortable 68px rows.
- Down each column, then across.
- Text labels for state and attention.

## Colors

GNOME colors are semantic system roles. Custom colors are user values. Neither is a fixed ASB palette, so the frontmatter has no fixed color primitives.

### Primary

Use GNOME `accent_color` for Pending text and unread dots. Working uses the GTK `success` style. Waiting text uses the Idle text roles (the user asked for this on 2026-10-23); its `?` or dot and its place at the top show that it waits. After **Mark read**, a Waiting row sorts with the Idle rows (the user asked for this on 2026-10-25). The current GNOME accent can be yellow; do not make that machine setting a permanent product rule.

### Neutral

Use the current GNOME window background and foreground. Inherit dim text, hover, focus, and control surfaces from GTK. Columns use the GNOME `borders` role for their divider. Provider marks use the foreground with reduced opacity (0.65). A provider mark is never lighter than its folder text: in GNOME colors a bright card draws its mark in the folder tone, `alpha(@window_fg_color, .55)` (`#9c9c9d` on `#222226`), also on hover and focus; custom colors keep 0.65, and the quiet and limited marks keep their rules (R3, 2026-11-04).

### Custom colors

The saved fields are `background`, `text`, `accent`, `muted`, and `divider`. Each is a six-digit `#RRGGBB` value. The background must have relative luminance at or below 0.12. Text, muted text, and accent must each have contrast of at least 4.5:1 against both the background and row highlight. Divider color has no minimum contrast.

The highlight mixes the background (85%) and accent (15%) in encoded RGB, with each channel rounded. Custom Working, Waiting, and Pending labels use the accent. Their text labels keep the states distinct. Validation errors retain the applied theme and saved file.

A user color change selects Custom colors. Apply checks, applies, and saves the palette with visible success or error text. Loading saved colors and resetting the pickers do not change the selected mode. Reset removes the saved palette and selects GNOME colors. Save errors keep the previous palette.

**The Local Theme Rule.** Apply colors only to ASB. Never change GNOME settings to match a preview.

## Typography

Inherit the current GTK font family and control typography. Do not add a font or record the current machine's font as a requirement. The frontmatter records only ASB's explicit row and state sizes. Control and caption styles remain native.

The board text follows the GNOME text scale (Settings > Accessibility > Large Text, or the text scaling factor): the scale is `gtk-xft-dpi / (96 × 1024)` from `Gtk.Settings`, 1.0 when unset (`read_text_scale()`). Every px font size of the board CSS (cards, row panel, tags, card tooltips, Filters, and the Preferences pet names) is its size at 1.0 times the scale: 13px is 16.25px at 1.25 and 19.5px at 1.5, and 11px is 13.75px and 16.5px. The mark glyphs (the `?` and `!` glyphs and discs, the passive `?`), the number keycap, and all icon and button sizes do not scale. A change of `gtk-xft-dpi` builds the board CSS again (V9, 2026-11-04). At scale 1.0 the board is exactly as before. The hover pocket follows the scale: it ends at the top of title line 2 (see **Session row**).

Compact keeps the title on one line. Comfortable shows the title in at most two lines (`CardTitle`, one widget that lays out two Pango lines with the CSS font and color). Line 1 keeps the 54px corner reserve and breaks at a word; line 2 runs to the right edge of the text column (the right edge of the age) and ends with `…` there. A two-line title has a 32px block. A one-line title has a block that ends where line 2 would end, so the state line sits right under it with the same gap as under line 2; the free line goes to the card bottom (V4 and V5 of round 2, 2026-11-04). Both ellipsize at the end and use the same native font family and 13px title size. A cut title ends with `…` right after its last visible character, with no white space before it (`Which tax region…`, not `Which tax region …`): the Compact title (`CompactTitle`) shows the longest start of the title that fits with `…` at its width, and Comfortable title line 2 is cut in the same way (`ellipsis_cut()`). The Compact title asks for the size of the full title, so the cut never changes the layout; it follows a text, style, or width change in an idle callback. The tooltip carries the full title and folder. The title from the server has at most 300 characters; a longer stored title ends with `…`, and whole Unicode code points are kept. Rows, tooltips, and search use this limited title.

## Layout

Column Flow uses equal-width native lists with a 1px vertical divider in the middle of each 12px gap (6px ±0.5px from the cards). Every divider runs from the top of the card area to the bottom of the tallest column, also beside a short last column. The pitch, card positions, divider drag, and hit area stay unchanged. Use the frontmatter spacing tokens for frame margins and columns. All real columns are in one horizontal native box, with no stacked pages or trailing empty columns.

Rows use a nominal 22px in Compact and 68px in Comfortable. These are minimum heights, and they are the real heights with the reference font, Cascadia Code NF 11. At a larger text scale the minimum heights grow by the growth of their measured Pango lines (`measure_card_heights()`: Compact one 13px line; Comfortable the two-line 13px title, the folder line, and the 11px state line), and when a card is still taller after layout, every card of the view takes that height; at 1.0 they stay exactly 22px and 68px. A font with higher lines makes rows higher, and a two-line title can make one row higher than the others. Packing uses the viewport of the scroll area, not the whole window and not the scrolled window: a horizontal scrollbar that is not an overlay takes height from the viewport. `columns = max(1, floor((width + 12) / (column_width + 12)))` and `capacity = max(1, floor((height - 4) / row_height))`, where `width` and `height` are the viewport size and `row_height` is the height of the highest visible row after layout. Before the rows have a layout, and at a view change, `row_height` is the nominal value. When the measured value is different, the window repacks one time. The shared target width defaults to 240px and accepts 160–600px. A 360px window has one default column or two at 160px. Fill down a column, then move across. Create only the needed columns and continue left to right. The horizontal scrollbar is automatic; vertical scrolling is disabled. Ordinary wheel movement maps to the horizontal adjustment; trackpad X and Shift-wheel work too. Keep the visible-column width logic and equal widths across the full strip. Columns keep the shared width when the list has fewer columns than the window can show, for example search results; long titles must not make a column wider.

Use one critically damped Libadwaita spring toward the target for wheel scrolling in both views. The damping ratio is 1 and the velocity is continuous. The spring settles in about 350 ms with no overshoot. A new wheel event keeps the current speed, limited so that the scroll never passes its clamped target. A move of less than half a pixel is immediate. Do not restart an ease-out for each wheel event. Keep the step per click. Same-direction input adds to the clamped target; reversal starts from the current visible position. Surface/trackpad input stays direct and cancels the wheel animation. GNOME animations off gives immediate wheel feedback. Bounds, layout, filters, view changes, scrollbar or focus scroll changes, unmap, and close cancel old targets. Unchanged refresh keeps active scroll. Add no permanent frame loop or source reads on animation frames.

The native mock captures show one, two, and four default columns at window widths of 360, 680, and 1040 pixels. A 1080×248 window shows four default columns or six at 160px, with three complete Comfortable rows per column. These are checked sizes, not fixed column-count breakpoints. The default window is 420 by 900 pixels.

Use one `Gtk.WindowHandle` toolbar row with icons only at every width. The ⋮ main menu and native Close stay fixed at the right. The 16px disc stays in a 20px slot at the left at widths of at least 320px, then the search button (`system-search-symbolic`). Below 320px, hide the disc and the search button. The center holds the Drawer and Filters pills with no text labels, then a horizontal native scroller for filter chips; active chips keep their text, because they show which filters are on. It never wraps to a second row. Use pointer scrolling and native keyboard focus to reach chips outside the visible center. At 680px or more, show Refresh as an icon before ⋮; below 680px, Refresh stays in Preferences > Sessions. While the board zoom is not 100%, a zoom badge (the user's choice C Badge, 2026-11-05) is the first button of the toolbar end: before Refresh when Refresh shows there, else before ⋮. It is a pill `Gtk.Button` with the toolbar button look (`asb-toolbar-button asb-zoom-badge`, 12px semibold tabular digits, padding 2px 8px) whose label is the level, such as `125%`, also at start with a saved zoom. It keeps its size at every zoom, never covers the window controls, and still shows at the narrowest toolbar. A click, Enter, or Space resets the zoom to 100%. Tooltip **Reset zoom to 100% (Ctrl+0)**; accessible name **Zoom 125%. Reset to 100%** with the current level. Each zoom change (Ctrl+wheel, Ctrl+plus, Ctrl+minus, Ctrl+0, or the badge) updates the label at once, rings the badge with a 2px inset accent ring (`@accent_color`, or the custom accent) for 400 ms, and announces **Zoom 125%** with medium priority where GTK has `Gtk.Accessible.announce`. A new change in that time starts the 400 ms again. A reset to 100% shows `100%` with the ring for 1.5 s, then the badge hides; a change in that time cancels the hide. The ring has no transition, so it also shows with animations off. The search field is never in the toolbar: at every width it is an inline row under the toolbar, a `Gtk.Revealer` that slides down in 150 ms (no motion when animations are off). The search button, typing on the board, and the Preferences search action open it, also below 320px. It stays while it has a query or the focus; with an empty query, it hides when it loses the focus. It is not a popover, so one click on a card opens the session while you search.

The ⋮ button is a `Gtk.MenuButton` (`view-more-symbolic`, tooltip and accessible label **Main menu**) with a `Gio.Menu` model: **Messages** (Ctrl+M) and **Usage** (Ctrl+U), a separator, then **Preferences** (Ctrl+,). GTK draws the separator between the two sections and shows each accelerator at the right of its item. The menu popover uses the Board palette like the other popovers. Ctrl+M and Ctrl+U work in every ASB window. Ctrl+W closes only Messages or Usage. Ctrl+Shift+U stays free for GTK Unicode input.

The Filters pill uses `.asb-filter-pill` and a drawn funnel glyph (`asb-filter-symbolic.svg`). It shows no text label at any width; its tooltip and accessible name keep the word Filters. The pill keeps its normal look when a popover filter is active: no accent and no number. The chips show the active filters. Accessible name is Filters; accessible description lists the active filters or No filters. A `Gtk.Popover` on the pill is 280px wide with its arrow. Its horizontal offset keeps it at least 8px inside the board; if it is wider than the available space, its left edge stays at 8px. The arrow still points at the pill. The scroll body has a 10px end margin to keep the rows and Include archived switch clear of the scroll bar. The pill tooltip is off while the popover is open and returns when it closes; its text does not change. It holds a Pending check row, a State section with four check rows, a Profiles section grouped by machine and app, and an Include archived switch. An app with one profile is one check row. An app with two or more profiles is a header check row with mixed state; a click turns all its profiles on or off. Footer Clear filters clears Pending, states, profiles, and archived, not Drawer or All cards. Escape closes the popover and focuses the Filters pill. The popover does not take the click outside it: a press outside closes it and also reaches the control under the pointer, so one click opens a card. It also closes when the keyboard focus leaves it and when the window stops being active. Only Escape and the pill return the focus to the pill. A press on the board in the place of the popover does nothing, also before the popover shows on the screen. While the popover is open, the chips do not change; they change when it closes, so the pill and the popover do not move under the pointer. Space and Enter turn the Include archived switch on or off (a popover has no Space binding of its own for a switch).

Drawer stays as a native toggle pill. Show no numeric counters or count summary in the main view. Drawer keeps its glyph and a plain 5px occupancy dot, with no text label at any width. The dot is visible when at least one drawer row passes the archive setting; profile, state, and search filters do not change it. Empty drawers hide it. The selected pill uses the accent background and an inverted dot. Keep the existing peek tooltip and describe an empty or occupied drawer through accessibility. Keep native move, resize, and close behavior and the outer border. **Preferences** in the main menu raises the retained Preferences window. List repacking does not rebuild Preferences controls or take their focus.

Each active filter value is one chip in a horizontal trail after the pills. Chip order is Pending, chosen states in STATES order, chosen profiles in popover order, Include archived, then All cards. A chip is one `.asb-filter-pill` button with an optional 8px profile color dot, a label ellipsized at 18 characters, and a 12px `window-close-symbolic`. A click or Delete/BackSpace removes only that value. Tooltip and accessible label read Remove filter: <full name>. A remote profile chip reads label · machine. After removal, focus moves to the next chip, else the previous, else the Filters pill. Chip add and remove use a `Gtk.Revealer` SLIDE_RIGHT 200ms; GTK animations off gives instant change. The chip scroller is its own `Gtk.ScrolledWindow` with `propagate_natural_width=True`, no hexpand, external scrollbar policy, and a 16px fade via `undershoot.left`/`undershoot.right` CSS nodes.

**Card limit.** The user approved the card limit on 2026-10-20 from `.impeccable/options/card-limit-2026-10-20/index.html`, with the More card from option A of `.impeccable/options/fewer-remote-cards-2026-10-20/index.html` (both git-ignored, only on the Debian PC). The board shows only the first N cards in board order (pins, then Pending, Working, Idle, and Unknown). N applies after all filters and search, to local and remote cards alike. Sessions > **Cards on the board** sets N (default 100). One More card comes after card N and stands for the rest. It is a normal card with these parts: the `view-more-horizontal-symbolic` mark, the title **More sessions**, the dim state **Show all**, and the age of the newest card that it stands for. Comfortable also shows the folder line **After the first N**, a dim 11px `go-next-symbolic` arrow after **Show all**, and the age as **from 11d ago**. Compact shows the age as **· 11d ago**. The More card has the normal hover and focus look. It has no profile badge, card actions, row panel, drag and drop, or number key. A click, Enter, or Space shows all cards, and the toolbar shows the removable chip **All cards ×** after the other filter chips. When the More card has the keyboard focus, the focus goes to the first card that it stood for; a click keeps the horizontal position. The chip × goes back to the first N cards. Show all is not saved: a new window shows the first N cards. The number of hidden cards is only in the tooltip and the accessible label, so the board keeps no numeric counters.

**The Whole Session Rule.** Keep provider, title, state, and attention within one activatable row. Compact uses one line; Comfortable uses one 68px Workspace-first row.

## Elevation & Depth

The session area is flat. It adds no row shadows or card surfaces. Dividers separate columns. GTK owns the toolbar, Preferences window, row panel, dialog, and focus depth. The row panel has a raised surface, a 1px edge, and a shadow (**Focus and row panel**); in custom colors every other ASB popover has a neutral 1px edge. Compact keeps static hover; keyboard focus keeps its existing native or custom highlight.

Comfortable paints one rounded hover highlight behind the cards and their actions, including across columns. One native animation moves it in 200 ms with exponential ease-out and fades arrival/leave in 100 ms, aligned with the approved command-palette motion reference. From hidden, it appears at the new card. A rapid pointer change starts from the current painted bounds. The paint takes no pointer input and cannot change card size or position. Use the current GNOME foreground at 7% opacity, or the existing custom highlight mix. With GNOME animations off, the highlight changes immediately. Clear it on repack, filters, resize, scroll, the start of a card motion, unmap, and close. During scroll, clear shared paint once and suppress hover restarts while the wheel spring is active. After scroll, ignore stationary or generated pointer motion. Real pointer movement restores the glide. Static row hover and keyboard focus remain while shared paint is absent. When cards move under a still pointer, the static hover moves to the card that is under the pointer after the layout. Keep keyboard focus and button feedback separate. Add no permanent animation loop.

Card motion applies to both views. When a new order or card set applies, each card that stays visible glides from its old place to its new place, and each new card fades in. A card that leaves disappears at once. The causes are a row that goes from unread to read, a new order from a refresh, a pin move, search text, the pills, the Preferences filters, and the archive setting. List changes from the drawer use this same motion; add no new timer and no frame loop for the drawer. Use one shared animation of about 350 ms with a critically damped spring feel for all cards. A new change during the animation starts from the current painted places. Use no motion at the first render, when the window is not mapped, when GNOME animations are off, or when the packing dimensions change (window size, column width, view). The motion is paint only: allocation, input, focus, and accessibility are at the final layout at all times. A pointer click during a glide opens a card only when that card is drawn under the pointer; else the click does nothing. Invalidate a column on each frame only when its motion bounds intersect the viewport. The final clear must also clear cached paint outside the viewport. Clear the shared hover highlight when a motion starts. Add no permanent frame loop. The board builds the cards of the columns that touch the view; the column on each side follows after the first frame; the other columns keep their width and get their cards when a scroll, a resize, or a keyboard move reaches them, before they paint.

Hold the card order and columns while the pointer is in the board, including the gaps, and its last real motion, button press, or scroll is newer than the last window key press. Current card content changes at once; a card filtered out stays in its place, new cards wait, and a session removed from the data leaves at once. End the hold after 1 s without real motion, counted from the later of the last real motion and the start of the hold, or at once on board leave or a window key press. Generated motion at the same pointer position does not extend it. A wheel step on a corner button (the mute or copy tag) is real motion. A click still opens the card under the pointer. When a held change glides another card under a still pointer, that card takes no press (no open, no corner button, no Read) until the pointer has real motion again; the card that the pointer was on before the change keeps its presses (R3, 2026-11-04). An open row panel holds order and card-set changes until it closes; then the pointer rule applies. Search, filters, Drawer view, Comfortable/Compact, window size, column width, Show all, and a pin move bypass and end the hold, also with a panel open. Apply held changes through the same render and 350 ms glide, with the existing animations-off and background rules. Keyboard focus stays on its card; number keys follow the cards as shown.

A message between two sessions shows as a short accent light. The light flies from the center of the sender card's provider icon to the center of the receiver card's provider icon, on a quadratic Bezier arc. The arc bows up for mostly horizontal travel and to the left for mostly vertical travel, by min(60 px, 0.22 of the distance). The light has a glow (radius 6 px, 25% opacity), a head (radius 3 px), and a tapered tail of 8 segments. The tail covers the last 20% of the flight time, so it is long when the light is fast and short when it is slow. The flight takes the distance divided by 1.5 in ms, from 480 ms to 900 ms, with cubic ease-in-out. On arrival, one ring grows from 5 px to 13 px around the receiver icon and fades out in 420 ms with exponential ease-out. The sender icon shows the accent color for the first 160 ms. Only a message from the last 30 s that ASB did not show before plays. The first dashboard after start plays nothing. Both cards must be visible, so search, filters, the drawer, and the archive setting hide the light. A message to or from a session on another machine plays between its remote card and the other card in the same way. One message plays for each sender and receiver pair in one update, and at most 6 lights fly at a time. When a card is outside the horizontal viewport, the light uses the viewport edge on that side, 2 px inside. A 3 x 18 px edge mark then fades in for 120 ms, stays for 300 ms, and fades out in 300 ms. When the sender is out of view, the edge mark flashes at the start. When the receiver is out of view, the edge mark flashes when the light reaches the edge, and no ring shows. When both cards are out of view, nothing plays. A light that comes during a card motion starts when the motion ends, between the final card places. A board window that is shown plays a new message also when another window has the focus. While the board window is minimized, hidden, or suspended (GTK reports `SUSPENDED`, for example when the compositor knows that other windows cover it), a new message plays no light, lights no card, and counts as seen, so it does not play later; a light that waits for a card motion does not play if the window is not shown when the motion ends. A light that already runs finishes. The paint is over the cards and takes no input. Use the custom accent color, or the GNOME accent color. With GNOME animations off, or without `Gsk.PathBuilder` (GTK 4.12), no light and no ring show; the provider icons of both cards show the accent color for 2 s. A card that becomes hidden drops its light. Clear all lights on unmap and close. Add no permanent frame loop.

Pets (Preferences > Appearance: Off, Crab, Pigeon, or Cat) replace the light with a pixel pet. Preferences shows four grouped picture tiles with one Tab stop and wrapping arrow keys, Home, and End. Each tile is 92 px high, 89 px wide at 420 px window width, and at most 112 px wide, with 8 px gaps. Each picture uses the board sprites at scale 2. Hover walks the pet at its `walkFps`; a choice crouches for 80 ms, hops for 160 ms (8 px high), then crouches for 80 ms. Off shows a still light glyph; hover flies the light for 700 ms every 1.2 s, and a choice flies it once. The checked tile keeps its background, with a 2 px inset accent ring and an 18 px check badge 6 px from the top and right edges. Below the caption, a full-width, 116 px stage has two 68 px Comfortable-style sample cards, with 12 px edge insets and a 12 px gap. It uses the board flight code and scale 2. A run starts 400 ms after the section shows or a choice, then every 4.6 s from start to start. Hiding the section or Preferences stops the run and removes its timeout. With GNOME animations off, the tiles stay still and both sample marks light for 2 s after a choice, with no flight or repeating timer. The user chose option C, Spawn, with all three pets on 2026-10-31 (`.impeccable/options/pets-2026-10-31/index.html`, git-ignored). The sprites are in `assets/pets/pets.json`. The pet walks on the floor line of each card (its painted bottom edge) and turns to face where it goes. Comfortable draws one sprite pixel as 2 x 2 px, Compact as 1 px. The phases: rise 380 ms (the pet climbs out of the sender card, cut at the floor line); a walk; for another row, a walk to 14 sprite pixels before the stop, crouch 80 ms, jump (260 ms + half the row distance, from 300 ms to 620 ms), and land 80 ms; then windup 120 ms, toss 240 ms, hop 160 ms (4 sprite pixels high), and dive 320 ms (cut at the floor line). The walk speed is the distance / 1.1 per second, from 120 to 300 px/s, but a walk takes at most 2000 ms. The jump rises 9 sprite pixels above the higher floor, but the pet stays 2 px inside the strip top. The pet stops 12 px before the receiver icon center in Comfortable (below the icon), and its front edge stops 12 px before it in Compact (level with the icon). The envelope flies from the paws to the icon center on a quadratic arc with its control point 9 sprite pixels above the higher end, with quadratic ease-out. The sender icon shows the accent color for 400 ms, the receiver icon for 360 ms from the arrival. The sender card gets an accent tint at 15% for 520 ms, the receiver card from 120 ms before the arrival to 200 ms after the dive; each tint fades in for 120 ms and out for 240 ms, under the cards. Accent particles (2 x 2 px) burst for 360 ms: dust at the sender floor, 8 sparkles at the icon on arrival, and dust at the dive. After the toss the paws are empty: no frame shows the envelope again. A pet plays only when both cards are inside the horizontal viewport; else the light plays with its edge mark. Pets and lights share the limit of 6. Pets need no `Gsk.PathBuilder`, so they also play on GTK 4.12. With GNOME animations off, both provider icons show the accent color for 2 s, as for the light. Its center and the passive `?` ink center stay within 0.5px of the Read slot center. On quiet cards its ink uses 75% of the quiet caption alpha, with a 3:1 floor on the background; hover restores its prior quiet caption color. Keep native shapes for buttons, entries, switches, dropdowns, color dialogs, and popovers.

The approved A Hollow dot for Discard has a 10px outer size: 6px empty space plus a 2px circular border, also in the tooltip. The cut-out handle keeps the surface color.

## Components

### Preferences

The user approved Option B: one separate, nonmodal native Preferences window. The toolbar settings button raises the retained window. Closing Preferences hides it, keeps ASB running, and keeps the selected section and form edits. Main-window shutdown destroys it. Use native window and control surfaces, with the same GNOME or custom theme as the main window.

Use a `Gtk.StackSidebar` on the left at wide widths. An `Adw.Breakpoint` at `max-width:600px` hides it and shows a section list first (the user chose option 1C on 2026-10-25 from `.impeccable/options/prefs-420-2026-10-25/index.html`, git-ignored): a `boxed-list` with one activatable `Adw.ActionRow` for each section and a `go-next-symbolic` suffix. A row opens its section page, with a flat **Back** button at the start of the header bar and the section name as the header title; the window title stays Preferences. **Back** or Escape goes back to the list, and Escape on the list closes Preferences. Each time the narrow layout starts, the list shows; going wide shows the sidebar and the current section. When the layout changes while the sidebar, the list, or **Back** has the keyboard focus, the focus goes to the sidebar row or the list row of the current section, and the section does not change. ## Do's and Don'ts

### Do:

- Do use native GNOME controls and semantic colors.
- Do keep a text label for each state.
- Do retain the chosen row height/layout and full tooltip details.
- Do validate custom colors before saving or applying them.

### Don't:

- Don't add a browser or webview to the native window.
- Don't treat all historical Idle sessions as Pending.
- Don't replace Working, Waiting, or Unknown with Pending.
- Don't pin a machine-specific theme, font, or accent as an ASB requirement.

## Refresh cadence

Native and web refresh runs every two seconds while any unarchived root session is Working, otherwise every five seconds. Use the complete list before filters. Keep this cadence when another app has focus. Local source-change events request a cached dashboard; they do not force a scan. Keep one read in flight and at most one follow-up. Both clients send the `ETag` of the dashboard that they show in `If-None-Match` and skip list updates on a `304` reply. Browser age labels still update once a minute through its existing refresh cycle. A background poll does not change the native Refresh button; make it insensitive only while a forced refresh is in flight or queued. Polling remains available if the event stream or file watch fails. Cold scanning and source writes can take longer than the interval. Activity-only observations do not rewrite ASB state.

A Comfortable card builds its limit clock, mute glyph, remote machine icon and name, and copied footer page on first use, in their usual places. The board creates column containers for the whole list, but starts a render with cards in at most the first two columns in view. Compact, which has about three times as many cards per column, and a view change start with one column. The rest of the view, then one column on each side, get cards one column per painted frame, in a separate default-priority idle turn after the frame and waiting input. While idle, the same queue fills up to one viewport width on each side, nearest columns first, then stops; it waits while a wheel glide runs. Show all keeps this bound. A scroll or a key that needs a column fills it at once and replaces the queued fill; a render, resize, search, or filter restarts the fill window. Keep native rows by session ID, including rows hidden by search or filters. Create a row only when first shown; release it when its ID leaves the full dashboard or the window closes. Keep focus and clock work limited to visible rows, stop Read feedback when a row is hidden, and update cached source markers when the palette changes. Update current data, action state, and time labels before showing a cached row. Skip unchanged cached text and action updates. Repack when the visible ID set, view, or packing dimensions change. Reuse detached column containers across filters, bounded by the full dashboard count and current row capacity; source removal releases excess containers. An order-only change moves only changed rows inside the current columns, preserving keyed widgets/actions and avoiding explicit menu/scroll teardown. The visible cards glide to their new places, and the shared hover highlight is cleared when that motion starts. While the board window is not active, the cards show their new places at once, as with animations off; a glide that already runs finishes. Keep focus when possible. Use surface layout signals with a single queued geometry check, not a permanent frame callback. A mode change can replace row content while retaining the row and its current action state.

Working time uses the current source task/request start only. Compact shows it after the state with a separator (`Working · 3m`), in the caption tone; Comfortable uses the right metadata label. No time shows seconds, in any window or client (the user asked for this on 2026-11-04): a Working time shows `<1m` in its first minute, then whole minutes rounded down (`12m`), then hours and minutes (`1h05m`); a reset countdown rounds up to whole minutes (`Resets in 1m` in its last minute, at most `23h59m` below one day), then `3d04h`; a mute time is `1h05m`, `1h`, or `52m`. The second unit of a two-unit time always has two digits. The card accessible name uses `spoken_duration` with the same rounded-down whole minutes: **Working time less than a minute.**, **Working time 1 minute.**, **Working time 12 minutes.**, or **Working time 1 hour 5 minutes.** It changes at the same minute boundaries as the visible time; the visible text and tooltip keep their short form. A local clock updates labels and accessible text without a data read; it wakes at the next whole minute of a Working time or a reset countdown, the next text change or end of a timed mute, else after 60 s (`clock_wait`), the same while the window is active or not. Tooltips use the current clock when queried. Idle age labels refresh once a minute. Unknown start and non-Working states have no running timer. The backend sends a stable `workingSinceMs`, not elapsed time.
